# M5.5 — Count/compare: scope, aggregation, threshold

*Design written 2026-07-24. Closes #38 (`StateCondition`), #39 (`CountSpec`),
#41 (counting cannot read projected state) and #34 (a player-scoped count reads
the wrong controller). Tracker: `tfausak/pawl#151`. Origin:
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-22-p9-target-filter-predicate-language-design.md`
§7, which deferred this concept rather than closing it.*

## 0. Why this phase, and what it proves

M4.5 P9 built a per-object predicate — `Pawl.Type.Filter`, evaluated by one
generic matcher that never learns which effect produced it. It deliberately
stopped short of the layer *above* the predicate: a **scope** (which objects,
over which zones, from whose perspective), an **aggregation** (how many; how
many *distinct card types*), and a **comparison to a threshold**. That layer is
this phase.

Two hand-carved types stand in for it today, one per customer:

- `Pawl.Type.StateCondition` — three variants, one per card, feeding the state
  trigger (CR 603.8), the intervening "if" (CR 603.4) and the "for as long as"
  duration (CR 611.2b).
- `Pawl.Type.CountSpec` — three variants, one per card, feeding
  `Quantity.Count`.

Six constructors, six cards, and every future card wanting a seventh. That is
the shape P9 retired for target predicates and it is retired here for counts.

The phase is not only a de-duplication. The two evaluators **disagree about the
projection**, and the disagreement is a real bug:

- `Event.stateHolds` reads the projection (`Projection.subtypesOf`,
  `Projection.controllerOf`). Correct — a subtype is CR 613.1d layer 4 and
  control is CR 613.1b layer 2.
- `Quantity.countOf` cannot. `Pawl.Projection` imports `Pawl.Quantity`, and
  `Quantity.evaluate` is called from *inside* the layer fold, so reaching the
  projection from there is a module cycle and a recursion at once.

One count vocabulary forces one answer, which is why #41 is inside this phase
rather than beside it.

### What this phase is *not*

It is **not** conditional effects. A real nonzero threshold in the wild —
Delirium's "four or more card types among cards in your graveyard", Metalcraft's
"three or more artifacts" — is almost always attached to an effect that does one
thing *or* another, and pawl has modal (M4g/M4h) but no conditional `Effect`
arm. Adding one would sprawl the phase into the open half. `Comparison` ships
with `AtLeast`/`AtMost` and no producer, tracked as an issue (§8).

It is **not** CR 613.8's dependency system. The layer bound this phase
introduces is a *terminating approximation* of it (§2.4), exact for every card
in the pool and honestly wrong in one stated case.

It is **not** CR 208.2a's "if the ability needs to use a number that can't be
determined … use 0 instead". Counting keeps returning `Maybe Integer` and
`Nothing` keeps propagating; the substitution is #65.

It is **not** a projection of non-battlefield objects. `Projection.gather` walks
the battlefield only, so a graveyard or hand candidate is matched against its
printed characteristics no matter who is counting (§8).

### Gate cards

- **Nightmare** — `{5}{B}`, Creature — Nightmare Horse, `*/*`. "Flying /
  Nightmare's power and toughness are each equal to the number of Swamps you
  control."
- **Sudden Impact** — `{3}{R}`, Instant. "Sudden Impact deals damage to target
  player equal to the number of cards in that player's hand."

Both oracle texts were read from Scryfall on 2026-07-24, not recalled.

Nightmare is the card this phase exists for. Its count reads a **subtype**
(layer 4) and **control** (layer 2) and feeds a characteristic-defining ability
at layer 7a (CR 208.2a, CR 604.3), so it cannot be answered without a projected
count — and both of its falsifiers are **already in the card pool**: Urborg,
Tomb of Yawgmoth makes every land a Swamp, and Blood Moon strips a nonbasic
land's types.

Sudden Impact is the card that kills a constructor named `CardsInYourHand`. Its
count is over the **target player's** hand — neither the resolving controller
nor the affected object's controller — so the perspective must become card data
rather than a caller-supplied argument. Set against Inner Calm, Outer Strength
(the same zone count, perspective = the resolving controller), one `Count` value
shape serves two different perspectives, which is the proof that the perspective
is a parameter of evaluation and was never part of the count's identity.

### The falsifiers, stated up front

- **The count is projected, not printed.** With Urborg on the battlefield,
  Nightmare's power equals the number of *lands* its controller controls, not
  the number of printed Swamps. This test fails today and cannot be made to pass
  without #41.
- **Layer 4 can take Swamp-ness away as well as give it.** Blood Moon turns a
  nonbasic Swamp into a Mountain with no other land types, and Nightmare's count
  drops.
- **The layer bound is ordered, not incidental.** Humility empties
  `characteristicPT` at layer 6, which is *before* 7a, so the count never runs
  at all and Nightmare keeps its base P/T. If the bound were wrong in either
  direction this test moves.
- **The perspective is the target, not "you".** Sudden Impact cast by a player
  holding five cards at a player holding two deals **two** damage.
- **`ControlledBy` still discriminates.** Nightmare ignores an opponent's
  Swamps.
- **Behaviour of the six existing cards is unchanged.** Barbarian Outcast,
  Sarcomancy, Master Thief, Tarmogoyf, Inner Calm Outer Strength and Khabál
  Ghoul keep their current observable behaviour across the migration; only their
  encoding changes.

## 2. Architecture

### 2.1 The types

Seven small types under `Pawl.Type.*`, one per module per the project's rule.

```haskell
-- Pawl.Type.PlayerRef
-- CR 400.1: "each player has their own library, hand, and graveyard. The other
-- zones are shared by all players." This says WHOSE zone a scope folds over.
data PlayerRef
  = EachPlayer                    -- every player's copy; the only meaningful
                                  -- value for a shared zone
  | Relative PlayerRelation       -- CR 109.5 / 102.2, against the context's perspective
  | InSlot SlotName               -- the player bound in a slot (Sudden Impact)
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)

-- Pawl.Type.EventShape
-- Which recorded events qualify. GameState.events is one-turn scoped by engine
-- choice (Engine.hs, at the turn change), so "this turn" is the log's extent and
-- needs no window here.
data EventShape
  = MovedBetween Zone Zone        -- CR 700.4: "dies" is battlefield -> graveyard
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)

-- Pawl.Type.Scope
-- What the count folds over.
data Scope
  = InZone Zone PlayerRef
  | InHistory EventShape          -- CR 608.2i: effects that look back in time
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)

-- Pawl.Type.Aggregation
-- CR 208.2a's Tarmogoyf counts card TYPES, not objects, so the aggregation is a
-- real axis and not always "length".
data Aggregation
  = Objects
  | DistinctCardTypes
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)

-- Pawl.Type.Count
data Count = MkCount Scope Filter Aggregation
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)

-- Pawl.Type.Comparison
data Comparison = Exactly | AtLeast | AtMost
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)

-- Pawl.Type.Condition
-- A predicate over game STATE rather than over an event: the state trigger
-- (CR 603.8), the intervening "if" (CR 603.4), and the "for as long as"
-- duration (CR 611.2b). Exactly one constructor -- there is no escape hatch.
data Condition = MkCondition Count Comparison Quantity
  deriving (Eq, Ord, Show)
```

The type graph is acyclic: `Count → Scope → Filter`, `Quantity → Count`,
`Condition → Count + Quantity`. The threshold is a `Quantity` rather than an
`Integer` because `Quantity` already exists and already composes; it costs
nothing and it is what a future count-vs-count comparison needs.

`Comparison` is a three-arm enum rather than a `Bool`-shaped "is zero", per the
no-boolean-blindness rule.

### 2.2 `Filter.IsSource`, and a two-field `Context`

`Filter` gains one atom and `Filter.Context` gains one field:

```haskell
data Filter
  = ...
  | IsSource                      -- the candidate IS the evaluation's source object

data Context = MkContext
  { perspective :: Maybe PlayerId -- CR 109.5's "you"
  , source      :: Maybe ObjectId -- the object the surrounding effect belongs to
  }
```

**This is not object identity leaking into the predicate language.** `IsSource`
relates the candidate to the evaluation *context*, in exactly the way
`ControlledBy PlayerRelation` already relates the candidate's controller to the
context's perspective. Neither atom carries a literal id; both are answered from
the context the caller supplies. `Pawl.Type.Exclusion` already names the source
this same way for target slots and affected sets, so the concept is not new to
the codebase — only its availability inside `Filter` is.

`IsSource` is what lets `StateCondition.YouControlSource` become an ordinary
count (§3) and lets `Condition` keep a single constructor.

`Exclusion` stays as it is. It is documented as a property of a *slot* (CR
601.2c's "another"), not of an object predicate, and re-expressing it as
`Not IsSource` is a separate refactor with no card driving it.

Six sites construct a `Context` today and all six are updated:
`Target.hs:55`, `Replacement.hs:271`, `Cost.hs:141`, `Projection.hs:199`,
`PlayerEffect.hs:147`, `Resolve.hs:531`. Five pass `Nothing`;
`Projection.affects` already has the source in scope and passes it for free.

### 2.3 The evaluation modules

Three modules, layered so that nothing is mutually recursive across a module
boundary:

```haskell
-- Pawl.Count  (imports Pawl.Filter, Pawl.Game, and Pawl.Type.*)
-- The characteristics of a candidate, as of whatever layers the caller has
-- already applied. Nothing when the candidate has no view.
type ViewOf = ObjectId -> Maybe Filter.View

evaluate :: ViewOf -> Filter.Context -> GameState -> Count -> Maybe Integer

-- Pawl.Quantity  (imports Pawl.Count) -- gains the ViewOf parameter
evaluate :: ViewOf -> Filter.Context -> GameState -> ObjectId -> Quantity -> Maybe Integer

-- Pawl.Condition  (imports Pawl.Count and Pawl.Quantity) -- new
holds :: ViewOf -> Filter.Context -> GameState -> Condition -> Bool
```

`Condition` evaluation cannot live in `Pawl.Count`: its threshold is a
`Quantity`, and `Pawl.Quantity` imports `Pawl.Count`. It gets its own module
above both. `Event.stateHolds` becomes `Condition.holds`, and `Pawl.Event` loses
its state-condition casing entirely.

`InSlot` needs no extra context field. The evaluator resolves it by reading the
source object's `Object.bindings`, exactly as `Quantity.X` already reads the
chosen X (CR 601.2b).

**Undeterminable resolves to false, and only in `holds`.** `Count.evaluate` and
`Quantity.evaluate` both keep returning `Maybe Integer`, propagating `Nothing`
as they do today. `Condition.holds` is total and must collapse it: an
undeterminable count, or an undeterminable threshold, makes the condition
**false**. That matches the behaviour of today's total `Event.stateHolds` and is
the conservative reading — a "for as long as" whose condition cannot be
evaluated ends rather than persists (CR 611.2b). CR 208.2a's "use 0 instead of
that number" is a different rule with a different scope and remains #65.

`holds` resolves the threshold by calling `Quantity.evaluate` with
`Context.source` as the object. Every current customer supplies one — a state
trigger's bearer (CR 603.8), an intervening "if"'s source (CR 603.4), a
`While`'s stored source (CR 611.2b) — so a source-dependent threshold
(`ManaValue`, `X`) is always answerable in practice; with no source it is
undeterminable and the condition is false by the rule above.

The injected `ViewOf` is the whole of the #41 fix. `Pawl.Count` never imports
`Pawl.Projection`; it asks its caller for characteristics and does not care
where they came from. That is the same injection shape M5c used for the subgame
runner (`resolveTopWith` / `noSubgame`), and it is the project's standing
preference for breaking a module cycle over an `hs-boot` file.

### 2.4 The layer bound, and why the recursion terminates

`Projection` gets one generalization; the existing entry points become wrappers:

```haskell
projectWith :: (Layer -> Bool) -> [Gathered] -> ObjectId -> GameState -> ProjectedCharacteristics
projectFrom   = projectWith (const True)
projectUpTo b = projectWith (< b)
```

`Layer`'s derived `Ord` **is** CR 613.1's application order — that is already
the only thing the projection sorts on — so `(< b)` is exactly "the layers
before this one".

While the fold applies layer `L`, the `ViewOf` it hands to `Count.evaluate` is
backed by `projectUpTo L`. Callers outside the fold — `Event`, `Expiry`,
`Stack`, `Resolve` — pass `\oid -> Just (Projection.viewOfObject oid gs)`, the
full projection.

**Termination.** A count evaluated while applying layer `L` sees its candidates
through `< L`. Any count encountered inside *that* nested fold is applied at
some layer `K < L` and therefore sees `< K`. The bound strictly decreases and
`Layer` is finite, so the nesting is bounded by the number of layers. The
obligation this places on the code is that a nested fold passes **its own**
current layer and never the outer one; that is asserted by a test, not merely
described in a comment.

**This is an approximation of CR 613.8, and it is wrong in one stated case.**
The comprehensive rules do not truncate: they evaluate against the current game
state and use the dependency system (CR 613.8a–c) to order effects, falling back
to timestamp order inside a dependency loop (CR 613.8b). pawl does not implement
the CR 613.8b reorder at all (#11). The bound is **exact** whenever a count
reads layers strictly earlier than its consumer's layer, which covers every card
in the pool and the overwhelming majority of real cards — counts read type and
control while their consumers sit at layer 7. It **under-reads** a count over
its own layer or later: a CDA at layer 7a counting "creatures with power 3 or
greater" sees no power at all, because `Filter.PowerAtLeast` is vacuously false
against a view whose power is `Nothing`. Filed as an issue and commented at the
bound (§8).

Nightmare lands inside the exact region: its CDA applies at
`Layer.CharacteristicPT` (7a), so its count sees layers 1–6 — control at 2 and
type at 4 — which is precisely what Urborg and Blood Moon change.

### 2.5 Whose "you" — the perspective inside the fold (#34)

`Projection.applyModification` supplies the **affected** object's controller as
"you". CR 604.3a(3) makes that right for a characteristic-defining ability — a
CDA does not affect other objects, so its "you" is the object's own controller,
and Nightmare depends on it. It is **wrong** for a static ability carrying a
player-scoped count: "creatures you control get +1/+1 for each card in your
hand" means the *source's* controller's hand (CR 109.5, "for a static ability,
this is the current controller of the object it's on"). Today `Gathered.gSource`
is dropped before `applyModification` sees it, so the right player is not merely
unused, it is unavailable.

The fix rides the `Context` threading this phase does anyway: `applyModification`
takes the gathered candidate's source, and the `Filter.Context` it builds carries
`perspective = controllerOf (gSource c) gs`. `applyCharacteristicPT` keeps
passing the object's own controller, because a CDA has no source object at all —
it is folded in place precisely because it is not a gathered candidate.

**Honesty about the gate.** Neither new card exercises this. Nightmare is a CDA
(the branch that was already correct) and Sudden Impact is a one-shot. #34 is
closed as the *defect* it describes — the wrong player is no longer supplied —
but the corrected branch has no producer in the pool, and that is filed as its
own issue (§8) rather than claimed as tested.

### 2.6 Three view builders, one `Filter`

| Candidate | Builder | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Battlefield / stack object | `Projection.viewOfObject`, layer-bounded inside the fold | CR 613: characteristics are the projection |
| Card in a library, hand or graveyard | `Projection.viewOfCard` (printed) | Nothing projects off the battlefield (§8) |
| An entry in the event log | new snapshot builder over the event's stored `ProjectedCharacteristics` | CR 608.2h last-known information |

The snapshot builder is what makes the history count correct where a printed
read would not be: a token has no printed card at all (CR 111.3), and a land
animated into a creature died *as a creature*. `Quantity.died` already reads the
snapshot for exactly this reason; the change is that the read now goes through
`Filter` instead of a hard-coded `Set.member Creature`.

The injected `ViewOf` is consulted only for the `InZone` scope. `InHistory`
builds its views from the log and ignores it.

### 2.7 Serialization

Added: tagged encoders and decoders for `Count`, `Condition`, `Scope`,
`EventShape`, `Aggregation`, `Comparison` and `PlayerRef`, plus the `IsSource`

Six card JSON files are rewritten (`barbarian-outcast`, `sarcomancy`,
`master-thief`, `tarmogoyf`, `inner-calm-outer-strength`, `khabál-ghoul`) and
two are added (`nightmare`, `sudden-impact`).

## 3. What each existing inhabitant becomes

| Old | New |
|---|---|
| `YouControlNo s` | `MkCondition (MkCount (InZone Battlefield EachPlayer) (And [HasSubtype s, ControlledBy You]) Objects) Exactly (Literal 0)` |
| `NoPermanentsOfSubtype s` | as above, filter is bare `HasSubtype s` |
| `YouControlSource` | as above, filter `And [IsSource, ControlledBy You]`, `Exactly (Literal 1)` |
| `CardsInYourHand` | `MkCount (InZone Hand (Relative You)) (And []) Objects` |
| `CardTypesInAllGraveyards` | `MkCount (InZone Graveyard EachPlayer) (And []) DistinctCardTypes` |
| `CreaturesDiedThisTurn` | `MkCount (InHistory (MovedBetween Battlefield Graveyard)) (HasCardType Creature) Objects` |
| Nightmare (new) | `MkCount (InZone Battlefield EachPlayer) (And [HasSubtype Swamp, ControlledBy You]) Objects` |
| Sudden Impact (new) | `MkCount (InZone Hand (InSlot "target")) (And []) Objects` |

`And []` is P9's existing identity predicate, so "no narrowing" needs no arm of
its own.

`YouControlSource` deserves a note, because it is the arm that looked like it
would not fit. CR 611.2b's "for as long as you control this creature" has two
halves: the source is still on the battlefield, and its projected controller
(CR 613.1b) is `you`. As a count, both fall out of one expression —
`count{battlefield, IsSource ∧ ControlledBy You} == 1` — because a source that
has left the battlefield is simply not in the scope. CR 400.7 keeps this robust
for free: a permanent that dies and returns is a new object with a new id, so
the stored source can never be satisfied by it.

**Retyped:** `Duration.ForAsLongAs Condition`, `TriggerCondition.StateIs
Condition`, `TriggeredAbility.intervening :: Maybe Condition`,
`Expiry.While PlayerId Condition`, `Quantity.Count Count`.

**Deleted:** `Pawl.Type.StateCondition`, `Pawl.Type.CountSpec`,
`Event.stateHolds`, `Quantity.countOf`, `Quantity.typesInAllGraveyards`,
`Quantity.died`.

### The `PlayerRef` wart, stated rather than hidden

`InZone Battlefield EachPlayer` reads as "each player's battlefield" when CR
400.1 makes the battlefield shared. A `Shared`/`PerPlayer` split in the type
would not actually prevent a nonsense pairing either — a card could still write
`Shared Hand` — so the flat shape stays and the existing `cardOffends` card-lint
family in `Pawl.CardSpec` rejects a shared zone paired with a non-`EachPlayer`
ref. Lint-enforced, not type-enforced, and filed (§8).

## 4. The two invariants

**The engine never cases on an effect's identity.** `Pawl.Count` cases on
`Scope`, `Aggregation` and `Comparison` — classifications, in the same family as
`CardType`, `Layer` and `Zone`. It never learns which effect or card produced a
count, and the six per-card constructors it replaces are exactly the identity
casing this phase removes. The net movement is *away* from the failure mode:
`Event.stateHolds`'s three-arm case, one arm per card, disappears.

**The engine never makes a player's choice.** No prompt is added, removed or
elided. Counting is a pure read of game state.

### Regression

The six existing cards keep their current assertions, unchanged, across the

### Evaluator-level tests

`Pawl.CountSpec` (new — the test-suite mirror of `Pawl.Count`, per the

## 7. Ordering within the phase (for the plan)

Each step is one small complete commit, test first.

1. `Filter.IsSource` and the two-field `Filter.Context`: type, matcher arm,
   codec, six call sites, tests. No count types yet.
2. The seven new types plus their codecs and round-trip tests. Unused.
3. `Pawl.Count.evaluate` with the injected `ViewOf`, plus `Pawl.CountSpec`
   against stub views. Still unused by the engine.
4. `Projection.projectWith` / `projectUpTo`; the layer bound and the
   `Filter.Context` threaded into `applyModification` and
   `applyCharacteristicPT`, including the §2.5 perspective correction (#34).
5. `Quantity.evaluate` takes the `ViewOf`; `Quantity.Count` switches payload;
   the three `CountSpec` cards migrate; `Pawl.Type.CountSpec` is deleted.
6. `Pawl.Condition.holds`; the four `StateCondition` customers migrate;
   `Event.stateHolds` and `Pawl.Type.StateCondition` are deleted.
7. `Subtype.Nightmare` / `Subtype.Horse`, then `nightmare.json` and its six
   tests.
8. `sudden-impact.json` and its three tests.
9. Issues filed and cited; #38, #39, #41, #34 and #151 closed.

Steps 1–4 are behaviour-preserving for the current pool — step 4's perspective
correction changes which player a static ability's count would read, and no card
in the pool has one. The first observable change is step 5.
