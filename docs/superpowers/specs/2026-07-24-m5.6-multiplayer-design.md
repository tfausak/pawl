# M5.6 — Multiplayer: general rules and free-for-all (CR 800, CR 806)

*Design written 2026-07-24, at M5.5's completion. Closes #143 (concede's priority
bookkeeping), #147 (a departed player revived by restart/subgame), #87 (CR 800.4g
— see §2.1 on that issue's misnumbering), #128 (CR 725.4 monarch reassignment),
and the multiplayer half of #148 (CR 103.5c); narrows #59 (`AttackTarget`) and
#138 (subgame non-winner set). Cashes `docs/design.md` §2.4, "Multiplayer from
the start." Tracker: an umbrella issue should be opened for this milestone before
the plan is written (§9).*

## 0. Why this phase, and what it proves

`docs/design.md` §2.4 is one sentence long and it is a bet:

> APNAP ordering, **801 (Limited Range of Influence)**, the monarch. A hardcoded
> two-player assumption is a rewrite, and it costs almost nothing to avoid up
> front.

Five milestones later, that bet has never been settled, because pawl has never
run a game with three players in it. Every `Pawl.Support` fixture is `alice` and
`bob`; every one of the ~2,000 `alice`/`bob` occurrences across 33 spec files
describes a two-player board. Four issues (#143, #147, #87, #128) were filed with
the same sentence — *recorded so the multiplayer work inherits it rather than
rediscovering it* — and each says the defect is unobservable at two players
because **a departure ends a two-player game before the next thing happens**.
That single fact is what has been hiding the drift.

This phase seats a third player and finds out what breaks.

### The headline finding: the drift is not where §2.4 predicted

§2.4 named APNAP ordering and the monarch, and the obvious prediction is that
`PlayerRelation.Opponent` — the "an opponent means the other player" assumption,
mentioned across a dozen library modules and flagged in its own type's doc
comment — is the largest single piece.

**It is not a piece at all.** §3.7 gives the evidence: all three sites that
resolve an opponent relation are already set-shaped and already correct for a
free-for-all. `Count.playersFor` returns `Just (filter (/= you) everyone)`;
`PlayerEffect.inScope` is the predicate `pid /= controller`; `Filter.matches`'s
`ControlledBy` arm is `c /= p` on a single candidate. None of them collapses to
"the other player" — that collapse happens in the *game state*, because there has
only ever been one other player. The doc comments claiming an "unstated
two-player assumption" are describing CR 102.3 (a **teammate** is not an
opponent), which is a *teams* problem, and teams are out of scope here.

Likewise APNAP: `Engine.apnapPlayers` genuinely rotates `turnOrder` from the
active player and filters to trigger controllers. There is no two-player
shortcut.

The real drift is in four places §2.4 did not name:

1. **Turn handoff** (`Engine.handoffTurn`) advances with `nextInOrder` over the
   raw `turnOrder` and never consults `Departure.stillPlaying` — so a departed
   player takes turns (CR 800.4k), and an `Expiry.AtTurnOf` on them never ends
   (CR 800.4m, #87).
2. **Priority around a departure** (`Engine.priorityLoop`) — #143, both halves.
3. **Leaving the game** is a `Player.status` flip and nothing else. CR 800.4a's
   entire object-removal sequence is unimplemented, so a departed player's
   permanents keep attacking, blocking, and generating continuous effects.
4. **Combat** picks the defending player with `defender : _` — the head of a list
   it silently truncates — and never performs CR 703.4h/507.1's turn-based
   action at all.

Plus two setup rules that are simply absent because they only exist in
multiplayer: CR 103.5c's free first mulligan (#148) and CR 103.8c/800.7's
"no player skips the draw step of their first turn."

So §2.4's claim is **half vindicated**. The data model — `turnOrder :: [PlayerId]`,
`players :: Map PlayerId Player`, `defendingPlayers :: [PlayerId]`,
`Departure.outcomeAfterLeaving`, `Count.playersFor`, `emptyGame :: NonEmpty
PlayerId -> GameState` — is N-player-shaped throughout, and nothing here is a
rewrite. The *control flow* around departure is two-player-shaped in four
concentrated places. That is a phase, not a milestone-sized excavation, which is
why this is an interstitial.

### What this phase is *not*

It is **not** CR 801 (limited range of influence). Range of influence is
**always unlimited** here — CR 800.1's default, and CR 806.2a's ("the limited
range of influence option usually isn't used in Free-for-All games"). Every rule
in CR 801 and every `…h`/`…e`/`…f` clause elsewhere that begins "In a multiplayer
game using the limited range of influence option" (CR 104.3h, 104.4e, 104.4f) is
therefore inert, and pawl implements none of it. **Saying so is the design
decision**: no range-of-influence field exists on `GameState` or `Player`, and no
site checks one.

It is **not** the multiplayer **options** (CR 802 attack multiple players, 803
attack left/right, 804 deploy creatures, 805 shared team turns) or the **variants**
beyond 806 (807 grand melee, 808 team vs team, 809 Emperor, 810 Two-Headed Giant,
811 alternating teams). Every one of those is a per-game toggle that pawl has
nowhere to read from — it has no format or variant concept, deliberately. Adding
one is a separate design (§8).

It is **not** teams. CR 102.3 ("a player's teammates are the other players on
their team, and the player's opponents are all players not on their team") and CR
102.4 ("your team") have no representation and need none in a free-for-all, where
CR 806.1 says players "compete as individuals against each other" and every other
player is an opponent by construction.

It is **not** formats. #148 has two halves, and only one is here — see §3.9.

It is **not** planeswalkers or battles. CR 508.1b's per-creature attack-target
announcement stays elided; §3.6 narrows #59 rather than closing it.

### Gate cards

All three are **already in the card pool**, and all three are gates precisely
because a two-player game cannot distinguish the right behaviour from the wrong
one. Oracle text read from Scryfall on 2026-07-24, not recalled:

- **Palace Jailer** — `{2}{W}{W}`, Creature — Human Soldier, 2/2. "When this
  creature enters, you become the monarch. / When this creature enters, exile
  target creature an opponent controls until an opponent becomes the monarch."
- **Master Thief** — `{2}{U}{U}`, Creature — Human Rogue, 2/2. "When this
  creature enters, gain control of target artifact for as long as you control
  this creature."
- **Silence** — `{W}`, Instant. "Your opponents can't cast spells this turn."

with **Mindslaver** — `{6}`, Legendary Artifact, "`{4}`, `{T}`, Sacrifice
Mindslaver: You control target player during that player's next turn." — as a
supporting falsifier for CR 800.4b.

**Palace Jailer is the card this phase exists for.** It is a Conspiracy card,
designed for multiplayer, and it lands on three of this phase's four axes at once:

- Its second trigger targets *a creature an opponent controls* — at three players
  that is a choice among two opponents' boards, and the engine must not make it.
- Its exile lasts *until an opponent becomes the monarch*. At two players that is
  a tautology (the only opponent becoming the monarch is the only way the crown
  can move off you). At three players it is a real predicate: either opponent
  taking the crown returns the creature; the controller retaking it does not.
- The monarch carries CR 725.2's two inherent abilities, one of which — "whenever
  a creature deals combat damage to the monarch, its controller becomes the
  monarch" — **only fires if the attacking player chose the monarch as the
  defending player**. That is CR 703.4h/507.1, the turn-based action pawl has
  never performed, and it is unobservable at two players where the choice does
  not exist.
- And CR 725.4 (the monarch leaving the game, #128) is reachable only when a
  departure does not end the game.

**Master Thief is the CR 800.4a gate**, and it is the shape of the rule's own
first example (Mind Control on Bianca's Assault Griffin). At three players, with
Alice controlling Master Thief and Carol's artifact:

- If **Alice** leaves, Master Thief is Alice's object and leaves the game, *and*
  CR 800.4a independently ends "any effects which give that player control of any
  objects" — the artifact reverts to Carol.
- If **Carol** leaves, the artifact is *Carol's* object and leaves the game; the
  continuous effect is orphaned rather than reverting anything.

Neither can be observed at two players: the departure ends the game first.

**Silence is the "each opponent" falsifier.** A naive "an opponent is the other
player" implementation silences one player. `PlayerScope.Opponents` must silence
both.

**No new card file is proposed.** That is itself a finding: this phase's gates are
all already in `data/cards/`, and the ones that would be tempting to add
(Cruel Edict for "target opponent", a landwalk creature for CR 802.2a's
"defending player") each drag in vocabulary this phase does not build (§8).

### The falsifiers, stated up front

Each of these fails today and cannot be made to pass without the corresponding
piece of §3.

- **A three-player lands-only game runs to a winner.** The existing
  `PropertySpec` property "a lands-only mirror always ends by deck-out" asserts a
  two-player game ends when the first player decks out. At three seats, the first
  deck-out must *not* end the game; a second must follow before there is a
  `Result.Won`. This one property exercises the seat walk, the priority handoff,
  departure, and the pass count simultaneously, and is the milestone's headline
  falsifier (§4).
- **A departed player does not take a turn, and their durations end anyway.**
  Alice, Bob, Carol; Bob gives himself an "until your next turn" effect and then
  Bob leaves. The effect must end at the seat where Bob's turn *would* have begun
  (CR 800.4m), not immediately and not never (#87). Today `handoffTurn` hands Bob
  the turn.
- **Priority after a concede goes to the next seat, not the first.** Alice, Bob,
  Carol in seating order; Bob concedes while holding priority; Carol receives
  priority (CR 800.4a's last sentence), not Alice (#143).
- **A departure does not decide a three-player game.** `Departure.outcomeAfterLeaving`
  already returns `Nothing` with two survivors; every *caller* that assumed
  otherwise (restart, subgame, mulligan, turn handoff) must agree (#147).
- **Master Thief's artifact reverts in one direction and vanishes in the other**
  (CR 800.4a), per the two cases above.
- **A departed player cannot be Mindslavered, and a Mindslaver controller leaving
  releases their victim** (CR 800.4b's last sentence, CR 800.4a's "effects which
  give that player control of … players end").
- **Attacking the monarch steals the crown; attacking the other opponent does
  not.** Palace Jailer + CR 725.2 + CR 507.1. Impossible to state at two players.
- **The monarch leaving passes the crown to the active player** (CR 725.4, #128).
- **Every player bottoms one fewer card on their first mulligan** (CR 103.5c) and
  **the starting player draws on turn one** (CR 103.8c / 800.7) — both only in
  the three-player game; the two-player assertions are unchanged.
- **Silence stops two opponents, not one.**

## 2. Two rules readings this phase must state out loud

### 2.1 #87 cites the wrong rule number, and the right one is CR 800.4m

Issue #87 is titled "CR 800.4g: an 'until your next turn' effect is not ended when
its player leaves the game." **CR 800.4g is not that rule.** Verified against
`docs/rules.txt`:

> **800.4g** If an object requires a player who has left the game to make a
> choice other than whether to pay a cost, the controller of the object chooses
> another player to make that choice. If the original choice was to be made by an
> opponent of the controller of the object, that player chooses another opponent
> if possible.
>
> **800.4m** When a player leaves the game, any continuous effects with durations
> that last until that player's next turn or until a specific point in that turn
> will last until that turn would have begun. They neither expire immediately nor
> last indefinitely.

The defect #87 describes is **CR 800.4m**. The lettering shifted when WotC
inserted clauses into 800.4; the issue was filed against a stale recollection of
the numbering. This is exactly the failure mode CLAUDE.md's "never trust recalled
Magic rules" exists to catch, and it is caught here rather than in code. Fix the
citation silently in the issue and use **800.4m** everywhere in the plan. CR
800.4g is separately deferred (§8) because it has no producer.

### 2.2 CR 806.2b says a free-for-all must pick an attack option — and pawl picks none

> **806.2b** Exactly one of the attack left, attack right, and attack multiple
> players options must be used. See rule 803 … and rule 802 …

Taken literally, "free-for-all with no options enabled" is not a legal
configuration. The scope for this phase nevertheless excludes 802 and 803, so the
honest statement is:

**pawl implements CR 506.2a/507.1's base turn-based action — the attacking player
freely chooses one of their opponents to be the defending player — which is the
rule that 802 *replaces* and 803 *restricts*.**

> **506.2a** During the combat phase of a multiplayer game, there may be one or
> more defending players … **Unless all the attacking player's opponents
> automatically become defending players during the combat phase, the attacking
> player chooses one of their opponents as a turn-based action during the
> beginning of combat step.** … That player becomes the defending player.
>
> **703.4h** Immediately after the beginning of combat step begins, if the game
> being played is a multiplayer game in which the active player's opponents don't
> all automatically become defending players, the active player chooses one of
> their opponents. That player becomes the defending player. See rule 507.1.

Call this configuration **"attack any one opponent."** It is the superset of
attack-left and attack-right (each of which *restricts* the same single choice to
one seat) and a subset of attack-multiple-players (which removes the choice and
makes every opponent a defending player at once). Landing 803 later is a filter
on `Combat.attackableOpponents`'s candidate list and nothing else; landing 802
later replaces `Combat.defender :: Maybe PlayerId` with a set and adds CR 802.4's
APNAP blocking. Neither is unpicked by choosing the base rule now — which is the
reason to choose it.

This is a **deliberate, stated divergence from CR 806.2b**, filed as an issue
(§8), not an oversight.

## 3. Architecture

Nine changes. Six are in the closed half's control flow, two are in setup, and one
is a documentation correction with tests behind it.

### 3.1 `turnOrder` is the seating order, permanently — the phase's load-bearing invariant

`GameState.turnOrder :: [PlayerId]` is today built once by `Setup.emptyGame` and
**never pruned when a player departs**; departure is a `Player.status` flip and
every consumer filters on read via `Departure.stillPlaying`. That behaviour is
accidental. This phase makes it a **committed invariant** and documents it on the
field:

> `turnOrder` is the **seating order** (CR 800.5, CR 806.3), rotated so the
> starting player is first (CR 103.1: "The game's default turn order begins with
> the starting player and proceeds clockwise"). It lists every player who *began*
> this game and is never shortened. Who is still *in* the game is
> `Departure.stillPlaying`.

Three separate rules depend on a departed player keeping their seat, which is why
this is the invariant everything else in §3 is built on:

- **CR 800.4m** needs the seat to know when the departed player's turn *would
  have* begun (#87 / §3.2).
- **CR 800.4a**'s last sentence — "priority passes to the next player in turn
  order who's still in the game" — needs the departed player's own position to
  find their successor (#143 / §3.3).
- **CR 729.1b**'s "the winner/loser of the subgame" needs the full starting
  roster to derive "each player who doesn't win" (#138 / §3.5).

The alternative — pruning `turnOrder` on departure — makes all three impossible
and buys nothing, since every read site already filters.

### 3.2 Turn handoff becomes a seat walk (CR 800.4k, CR 800.4m; closes #87)

Today:

```haskell
handoffTurn = State.modify' $ \gs ->
  let newActive = nextInOrder (GameState.turnOrder gs) (GameState.activePlayer gs)
   in Expiry.dropAtHandoff $ gs { GameState.activePlayer = newActive, … }
```

`nextInOrder` is the raw cyclic successor: a departed player becomes the active
player. And `Expiry.dropAtHandoff` reads `GameState.activePlayer` *after* the
update, so it can only ever end durations belonging to the seat that actually got
the turn.

Both rules are about the same walk:

> **800.4k** If a player who has left the game would begin a turn, that turn
> doesn't begin.

The handoff therefore walks the seating order one seat at a time from the current
active player. **At every seat it passes, the "that player's next turn begins"
event fires for expiry purposes**; only when the seat's player is still playing
does the turn actually begin there.

```
handoffTurn:
  for each successive seat s after the current active player, at most
  |turnOrder| times:
    gs <- Expiry.dropAtTurnOf s gs        -- CR 611.2a for the taker,
                                          -- CR 800.4m for every skipped seat
    if s is still playing:
      begin s's turn (activePlayer, turnNumber, events, phase/remaining,
                      activeControl/pendingControl promotion) and stop
    else:
      continue                            -- CR 800.4k: that turn doesn't begin
  fallback: keep the current active player (total; unreachable while the
            game is running, since a game with no survivors has a Result)
```

`Expiry.dropAtHandoff :: GameState -> GameState` is replaced by
`Expiry.dropAtTurnOf :: PlayerId -> GameState -> GameState`, which drops
`Expiry.AtTurnOf p` for the *given* `p` rather than for whoever happens to be
active. That one generalization is the whole of #87's fix; the seat walk supplies
the callers. `AtCleanup`, `Never` and `While` are untouched.

**Termination and totality.** The walk is bounded by `length turnOrder`, so it
terminates even with every seat departed; the fallback keeps the function total
with no partial `head`. Per CLAUDE.md this must be written as an explicit bounded
fold, not `cycle`/`head`.

**CR 800.4b interacts here.** `pendingControl → activeControl` promotion must
check the promoted `Decider`'s player is still playing:

> **800.4b** … If a player would be controlled by a player who has left the game,
> they aren't.

so a `Map.lookup newActive (pendingControl gs)` whose `Decider` names a departed
player yields `Nothing` (and the stale entry is dropped).

### 3.3 Priority around a departure (closes #143; CR 800.4a, CR 800.4j)

**`nextStillPlaying` looks the seat up in the full order.** Today it filters
`turnOrder` down to still-playing players *and then* asks `nextInOrder` for the
successor of `pid` — so when `pid` has already departed (which is exactly the
case the concede branch calls it in), `dropWhile (/= pid)` finds nothing and it
falls through to the head of the list. Restated against CR 800.4a's last
sentence:

> **800.4a** … If the player who left the game had priority at the time they
> left, priority passes to the next player in turn order who's still in the game.

```haskell
-- CR 800.4a: the seat is looked up in the FULL seating order (§3.1), so a player
-- who has already departed still has a position from which to find their
-- successor. Total: falls back to `pid` when no one is still playing.
nextStillPlaying :: GameState -> PlayerId -> PlayerId
```
— find `pid`'s index in `GameState.turnOrder`, scan forward cyclically, return
the first player in `Departure.stillPlaying`. This fixes #143's first half
without reordering the concede branch, and it is simultaneously the correct
implementation for the ordinary pass case.

**`passes` is reset to 0 on a concession** (#143's second half). The reasoning is
worth recording, because the naive reading says it is unnecessary:

- pawl only ever offers `Prompt.Concede p` to the *current priority holder*, so
  the conceding player has not passed this cycle. `passes >= length (stillPlaying
  gs)` therefore stays arithmetically consistent without a reset, and #143's
  "resolve a pass early" framing does not survive that observation.
- **But** once CR 800.4a is implemented (§3.4), a concession *removes objects
  from the game* — permanents leave the battlefield, spells leave the stack.
  CR 117.4's "if all players pass in succession (that is, if all players pass
  **without taking any actions in between**)" is then no longer satisfied by the
  earlier passes: an action was taken and the board changed.
- The asymmetry of the mistake decides it. Resetting costs at most one redundant
  pass prompt — and passing is always legal, so no choice is invented. Not
  resetting risks resolving a spell that a player, having just watched a board
  disappear, would have responded to — the engine making a decision for them,
  which is the invariant that outranks the plan.

Reset. The CR is genuinely ambiguous here and this is listed in §11.

**CR 800.4j: the turn continues without an active player.**

> **800.4j** If a player leaves the game during their turn, that turn continues
> to its completion without an active player. If the active player would receive
> priority, instead the next player in turn order receives priority, or the top
> object on the stack resolves, or the phase or step ends, whichever is
> appropriate.

`GameState.activePlayer :: PlayerId` is **not** widened to `Maybe`. The turn still
*belongs* to that seat — CR 800.4m's durations and CR 101.4's APNAP anchor both
reference it — and making it `Maybe` would ripple through every consumer to
express something none of them needs. Instead one helper:

```haskell
-- CR 800.4j: whoever actually receives priority when the turn's owner has left.
priorityHolder :: GameState -> PlayerId
priorityHolder gs =
  let active = GameState.activePlayer gs
   in if List.elem active (Departure.stillPlaying gs)
        then active
        else nextStillPlaying gs active
```

used at the two sites that today write `Just (GameState.activePlayer …)`:
`priorityLoop`'s entry and the post-resolution re-grant.

**Turn-based actions guard on `stillPlaying`.** Untap (CR 703.4c), draw (CR
703.4d) and cleanup discard (CR 703.4n) all belong to the active player. After CR
800.4a they own nothing and have no library, so untap is vacuous, but the draw
must be skipped explicitly rather than drawing from a missing library.

**APNAP intersects with `stillPlaying`.** `Engine.apnapPlayers` rotates the full
`turnOrder`; add the still-playing filter. This is belt-and-braces given CR
800.4d (§3.4) keeps a departed player's triggers off the stack in the first place,
but the rotation is the one place a departed seat could still be handed a
`Prompt.OrderTriggers`.

### 3.4 Leaving the game takes objects with it (CR 800.4a–e)

`Departure.leaveGame` currently flips `Player.status` and settles CR 104.2a. It
grows into CR 800.4a's ordered sequence. The rule's own ordering ("Then … Then …")
is load-bearing and the implementation follows it literally:

> **800.4a** When a player leaves the game, all objects (see rule 109) owned by
> that player leave the game and any effects which give that player control of
> any objects or players end. Then, if that player controlled any objects on the
> stack not represented by cards, those objects cease to exist. Then, if there
> are any objects still controlled by that player, those objects are exiled.
> **This is not a state-based action. It happens as soon as the player leaves the
> game.**

1. **Objects owned by the departed player leave the game.** Delete their
   `ObjectId`s from `GameState.objects` and from every zone that can hold them:
   the per-player `library`/`hand`/`graveyard` maps (drop the key), the
   `battlefield`/`exile`/`command` sets, the `stack` list, `Combat.attackers` /
   `Combat.blockers` / `Combat.struckFirst`, `exiledUntilMonarch`,
   `delayedTriggers`, and any `continuousEffects` / `replacements` /
   `playerEffects` whose source object is among them.
2. **Effects giving that player control of objects or players end.** Drop
   `ContinuousEffect`s whose *controller* is the departing player and whose
   effect is a control change (Master Thief, Act of Treason — both in the pool),
   and clear `activeControl` / `pendingControl` entries whose `Decider` names
   them (Mindslaver). Step 1 would eventually kill the Master Thief effect via
   its `ForAsLongAs` condition sweep, but 800.4a is **immediate** and a sweep is
   not; doing it explicitly is the difference between the rule and an
   approximation of it.
3. **Non-card objects on the stack they controlled cease to exist.** Their
   *spells* are cards and went in step 1; their *ability* objects
   (`Source.OfAbility`) are removed here.
4. **Objects still controlled by them are exiled.** Computed from
   `Projection.controllerOf` after steps 1–3. No card in the pool can produce a
   survivor of this sweep (Bribery is the rule's example and is not in the pool),
   so this arm is implemented and asserted empty rather than gated by a card.
5. **Status flip + CR 104.2a settle** — the existing `depart` /
   `outcomeAfterLeaving`, unchanged.
6. **CR 725.4 monarch reassignment** (§3.8) — the rule says "at the same time as
   that player leaves the game," so it belongs in this sequence, not after it.

Ordering note: steps 1–4 must run **before** the status flip, because they read
"objects controlled by that player" and `Projection` may consult status.

Three of the follow-on clauses are in scope because the pool can reach them:

- **CR 800.4b** — "If an object would change to the control of a player who has
  left the game, it doesn't … If a player would be controlled by a player who has
  left the game, they aren't." Guarded at the control-change resolution site and
  at the `pendingControl` promotion (§3.2).
- **CR 800.4d** — "If a triggered ability that would be controlled by a player
  who has left the game would be put onto the stack, it isn't put on the stack."
  One filter in `Engine.placePendingTriggers`.
- **CR 800.4e** — "If combat damage would be assigned to a player who has left
  the game, that damage isn't assigned." One filter in `Damage`. Reachable: a
  defending player can concede between declare-attackers and the combat damage
  step.

800.4c, 800.4f, 800.4g, 800.4h and 800.4i have no producer in the pool and are
deferred with stated triggers (§8), not silently skipped.

### 3.5 `Result` is not widened, and that is the finding

`Pawl.Type.Result` stays `Won PlayerId | Drawn`. The brief asked whether it needs
widening because "a player can lose while the game continues"; the answer, checked
against the rules, is no:

> **104.1** A game ends immediately when a player wins, when the game is a draw,
> or when the game is restarted.
>
> **104.2a** A player still in the game wins the game if that player's opponents
> have all left the game.
>
> **104.4a** If all the players remaining in a game lose simultaneously, the game
> is a draw.

`Result` is the **game's** outcome, and in a free-for-all with unlimited range of
influence there is still exactly one of them: one winner, or a draw. (The rules
that split the outcome per player — 104.3h, 104.4e, 104.4f — are all
range-of-influence rules and all out of scope; 104.2c/104.3g/104.4d/104.4g are
teams.) `Departure.outcomeAfterLeaving` is already written for N players and needs
no change:

```haskell
outcomeAfterLeaving leaving gs = case stillPlaying gs of
  [winner] -> Just (Result.Won winner)
  []       -> if null leaving then Nothing else Just Result.Drawn
  _        -> Nothing
```

What multiplayer changes is not `Result`'s shape but **the assumption, held by its
callers, that a departure decides the game**. The per-player outcome already
exists and already carries a reason: `Player.status = Departed Departure`, with
`Departure = Lost | Conceded | Drew`.

**#138 (subgame non-winner set) stays out of scope, narrowed.** Its *derivation*
becomes free here: because `turnOrder` is the permanent seating roster (§3.1), CR
729.1b's "each player who doesn't win the subgame" is
`filter (/= winner) (GameState.turnOrder subgameState)` — the full starting
roster minus the winner, exactly right. What is still missing is open-half
vocabulary: binding a *set* of players to a slot, and a per-player quantity
("half their life, rounded up"). Both are card-driven on full Shahrazad. The
issue should be edited to say so rather than left describing a derivation problem
that no longer exists.

### 3.6 Combat: the defending player becomes a real turn-based action (narrows #59)

Today `Combat.declareAttackers` does `case defendingPlayers gs of defender : _ ->`
— it takes the head of a list and drops the rest on the floor — and
`Combat.declareBlockers` loops over *all* of `defendingPlayers`, which at three
players would ask an opponent who is not being attacked to declare blocks.
Neither matches the rules once there is more than one opponent.

**New state.** `Pawl.Type.Combat` gains one field:

```haskell
-- CR 506.2/507.1: the one player being attacked this combat phase, chosen as a
-- turn-based action at the beginning of combat (CR 703.4h). Nothing before that
-- action and after CR 511.3's end-of-combat reset. Becomes a set if CR 802
-- (attack multiple players) is ever enabled.
defender :: Maybe PlayerId
```

`Combat.emptyCombat` sets it to `Nothing`; `Combat.clearCombat` resets the whole
record as it does today.

**New turn-based action**, at the beginning of combat step, before any trigger
(CR 703.4h is "immediately after the beginning of combat step begins"):

```haskell
-- CR 507.1 / 703.4h.
chooseDefender :: Game ()
```
reading `Combat.attackableOpponents :: GameState -> [PlayerId]` (CR 506.2a's
candidates: `Departure.stillPlaying` minus the active player), and writing
`Combat.defender`.

**New prompt.**

```haskell
ChooseDefender :: Decider -> PlayerId -> NonEmpty PlayerId -> Prompt PlayerId
```

`NonEmpty` because the action only runs when there is at least one candidate; the
empty case (no opponents left) cannot arise while the game is running and is
handled by not performing the action, leaving `defender = Nothing` and therefore
no legal attackers.

**Elision.** When the candidate list is a singleton the prompt is skipped. This is
not a deferred elision needing an expiry — it is CR 507.1 with one legal answer,
and CLAUDE.md's rule is "where the rules leave nothing to ask, don't prompt." It
is also what keeps every existing two-player test byte-identical.

**Consumers.**

- `declareAttackers` reads `Combat.defender` instead of pattern-matching a list
  head; `Nothing` means no attack is possible. Every attacker still gets
  `AttackTarget.OfPlayer` that one player: CR 508.1b requires a per-creature
  announcement only "if the defending player controls any planeswalkers, is the
  protector of any battles, or the game allows the active player to attack
  multiple other players" — none of which is true here.
- `declareBlockers` iterates `[defender]`, not `defendingPlayers` (CR 509.1:
  "**the** defending player declares blockers"). CR 802.4's APNAP loop over
  several defending players arrives with 802.
- `Combat.defendingPlayers` is **deleted** — no API stability obligations. Its
  two meanings split into `Combat.attackableOpponents` (candidates) and the
  `Combat.defender` field (the chosen one).

**#59 is narrowed, not closed.** Its first half — "which opponent" — becomes a
real prompt here. Its second half — which player/planeswalker/battle *each
creature* attacks (CR 508.1b) — stays elided, with the expiry retargeted to
planeswalkers, battles, or CR 802.

### 3.7 The `Opponent` axis is already correct; the work is proving it and fixing the comments

All three resolution sites, verbatim:

| Site | Code | Free-for-all reading |
|---|---|---|
| `Pawl/Count.hs` `playersFor` | `PlayerRelation.Opponent -> Just (filter (/= you) everyone)` | Every other player. Correct. |
| `Pawl/PlayerEffect.hs` `inScope` | `PlayerScope.Opponents -> pid /= controller` | Membership predicate, applied across all players. Correct. |
| `Pawl/Filter.hs` `matches` | `PlayerRelation.Opponent -> c /= p` | Per-object controller test. Correct. |

Each carries a comment saying it "carries an unstated two-player assumption."
That is **half true and misleading**: the reading is exactly right for a
free-for-all (CR 806.1, players compete as individuals; every other player is an
opponent) and wrong only for CR 102.3's team games. The comments are rewritten to
say that, with a teams issue cited.

The work in this phase is therefore three tests, not a refactor:

- **Silence** (`PlayerScope.Opponents`) stops *both* opponents.
- **Palace Jailer**'s target pool (`Filter.ControlledBy Opponent` over
  `Pool.Creatures`) spans *both* opponents' creatures, and `Prompt.ChooseTargets`
  offers all of them.
- A `Count` with `Scope.InZone z (PlayerRef.Relative PlayerRelation.Opponent)`
  folds over *two* players' zones.

**What is genuinely missing is narrowing a player target.** `Target.legalRecipients`
says so at the code site: *"A Filter ranges only over objects, never players, so a
`ToPlayer` recipient is never narrowed."* So `TargetSpec (MkTargetSpec Pool.Players
…)` cannot express "target **opponent**" — only "target player." At two players
those coincide for every card that has both readings; at three they do not.

This is **deferred, card-driven**, because no card in the pool prints "target
opponent." The shape it will take is recorded here so the next person does not
redesign it: `Filter.View` gains a `player :: Maybe PlayerId` field (populated for
a `Recipient.ToPlayer` candidate, `Nothing` for an object view), and `Filter`
gains one atom `IsPlayer PlayerRelation` — context-relative in exactly the way
`ControlledBy` and `IsSource` already are, carrying no literal id. Expiry trigger:
the first card printing "target opponent" (Cruel Edict is the canonical cheap
one). Note this is **additive** — it does not disturb `Pool`, whose doc explicitly
says it grows "only when the rules define a new kind of targetable object," and an
opponent is not one.

### 3.8 The monarch (CR 725.4; closes #128)

> **725.4** If the monarch leaves the game, the active player becomes the monarch
> at the same time as that player leaves the game. If the active player is
> leaving the game or if there is no active player, the next player in turn order
> who can become the monarch becomes the monarch. If no player still in the game
> can become the monarch, the game continues with no monarch.

`Monarch.reassignOnDeparture :: PlayerId -> GameState -> GameState`, called as
step 6 of §3.4's sequence:

- If the departing player is not the monarch, nothing happens.
- Otherwise: the active player, if still playing and not the one leaving; else the
  next seat in `turnOrder` who is still playing (§3.1 gives the seat walk);
  else `Nothing`.

"who *can* become the monarch" is read as "is still in the game" — no card in the
pool prevents a player from becoming the monarch, and the phrase exists for cards
that do. Noted at the code site.

Two consequences that fall out and are asserted rather than built:

- `Monarch.inherentMonarchPending` can never fire for a departed player, because
  725.4 guarantees the monarch is always still playing.
- `Monarch.returnExiledForMonarch`'s `Map.filter (/= m)` — "an opponent of the
  entry's controller is the monarch" — is **already** correct in a free-for-all,
  for the same reason as §3.7: monarch ≠ controller ⟺ an opponent is the monarch.

One edge is deferred (§8): if the *controller* of a Palace Jailer effect departs
while a creature is still exiled under it, the `exiledUntilMonarch` entry names a
player who is no longer in the game, and the CR gives no direct answer.

### 3.9 Setup: mulligans, the first draw, restart, and subgames

**CR 103.5c / 800.6 — the free first mulligan (multiplayer half of #148).**

> **103.5c** In a multiplayer game and in any Brawl game, the first mulligan a
> player takes doesn't count toward the number of cards that player will put on
> the bottom of their library or the number of mulligans that player may take.
> Subsequent mulligans are counted toward these numbers as normal.

`Pawl.Mulligan.takeMulligan` today computes `n = min (fromIntegral count) (length
newHand)` where `count` is the number of mulligans taken. (For the record: this is
a correct London mulligan — always redraw a full hand, bottom `count` — not, as is
easy to misread, a Vancouver one.) The change is one saturating subtraction, and
the number subtracted comes from **one named function** rather than an inlined
player-count test (§3.11):

```haskell
-- CR 103.5c / CR 800.6: how many of a player's mulligans are free -- i.e. do not
-- count toward the number of cards bottomed or the mulligan limit.
--
-- CR 800.1: "A multiplayer game is a game that BEGINS with more than two
-- players" -- not one that currently has them. GameState.turnOrder is the
-- permanent seating roster (§3.1), so it answers "began with" directly, and a
-- game that drops to two players by departure keeps its free mulligan.
--
-- CR 903.12g grants the same allowance for a different reason (any Brawl game).
-- Nothing in pawl can be a Brawl game today, so this is the only cause; a Brawl
-- layer redefines THIS function rather than chasing call sites.
freeMulligans :: GameState -> Natural
freeMulligans gs = if length (GameState.turnOrder gs) > 2 then 1 else 0
```

and the bottom count becomes `count` less `freeMulligans gs`, clamped at zero —
written as an explicit `case` on the comparison, not with `Natural` subtraction,
which is partial below zero.

**Why a `Natural` and not a `Bool`.** Not because a value of 2 is coming: there
are exactly **three** free-mulligan rules in the CR — CR 103.5c, CR 800.6 and CR
903.12g — and all three carry identical wording, *"the **first** mulligan a player
takes doesn't count toward…"*. No rule anywhere grants two. (CR 902.5a's Vanguard
clause is about the *size* of a multiplayer first mulligan, not about a second free
one, so it is not a fourth.) The type admits 2; nothing produces it. The two
reasons that do hold:

1. **CLAUDE.md forbids boolean blindness** — "a custom sum type beats a bare
   `Bool`." This is the textbook instance. `freeFirstMulligan gs == True` carries
   no information about what to *do* with it; the caller has to already know the
   convention that `True` means "subtract one." A `Natural` says what it is.
2. **The call site is subtraction.** The bottom count is `count` less the free
   allowance, so a `Natural` is literally the type of the thing being subtracted.
   A `Bool` forces an `if free then 1 else 0` conversion at every use — the
   conversion being the tell that the `Bool` was the wrong type.

The mulligan **limit** needs no separate change: pawl expresses CR 103.5's "a
player can take mulligans until their opening hand would be zero cards" by
treating an empty hand as a forced keep, and with one free mulligan the hand
reaches zero after eight rather than seven. That is exactly what 103.5c's second
clause asks for, and it falls out.

Two edges worth stating: a **restarted** game (CR 727.1) begins with whoever was
in the ended game, so a 3-player game that restarts with 2 survivors is *not* a
multiplayer game for 103.5c's purposes — reading `turnOrder` of the *new* game
gets this right for free. A **subgame** (CR 729.2) has the same roster as its
parent and inherits the same answer.

**The Brawl half of #148 is out of scope**, and #148 should be **split**: the
multiplayer clause closes here; the Brawl clause needs a format/variant concept
that pawl does not have and that this phase deliberately does not introduce (§8).

**CR 103.8c / 800.7 — the starting player does not skip their first draw.**

> **103.8a** In a two-player game, the player who plays first skips the draw step
> of their first turn.
>
> **103.8c** In all other multiplayer games, no player skips the draw step of
> their first turn.

`Engine.skipsDraw` is today `turnNumber == 1 && head turnOrder == activePlayer`.
It gains `&& length (GameState.turnOrder gs) <= 2`. Small, easy to miss, and a
real observable difference in every three-player game from turn one — which is why
it is called out rather than left to be discovered.

This is the phase's **second** capability seam, and it is already correctly
shaped: `Engine.skipsDraw :: GameState -> Bool` (`Engine.hs:154`) is one named
function with exactly one call site (`Engine.hs:171`,
`skip <- State.gets skipsDraw; Monad.unless skip (Event.drawCard active)`), so the
player-count test goes *inside* it and CR 103.8b's Two-Headed Giant clause — the
same capability, granted to a *team* for a third reason — would later be another
arm of the same function. Nothing else needs to know (§3.11).

**`skipsDraw` stays a `Bool`, and that is not inconsistent with the paragraph
above.** The no-boolean-blindness rule is about values that lose information, and
the two cases differ in kind:

- `freeMulligans` returns an **operand of arithmetic**. Its consumer subtracts it.
  A `Bool` there is blind because `True` does not say *what to subtract*.
- `skipsDraw` returns a **proposition, and its name is that proposition**.
  `Monad.unless skip …` reads as English at the one site that consumes it, and
  CR 103.8a/103.8b/103.8c partition the space exhaustively into skip / don't-skip
  with no third state a future rule could add.

The codebase already settles this: `Filter.matches`, `Target.stillLegal` and
`Condition.holds` are all `Bool`-returning predicates named as propositions.
Introducing `data DrawStep = Skips | Draws` would cost a new `Pawl.Type.*` module
(the one-type-per-module rule) plus a codec-free but still real type, to be
consumed by a single `unless`, and would make `skipsDraw` the odd one out among
the engine's predicates. **Decision: leave it alone.** Recorded here rather than
changed silently in either direction.

*(Housekeeping for the plan, not a design point: the comment above `skipsDraw`
cites "CR 103.7a", as do `Setup.hs:244` and `GameSpec.hs:578`. CR 103.7 is
Planechase; the rule is **CR 103.8a**. Fix the three citations while this function
is being edited.)*

**CR 727.1 / 729.2 — rebuild from the still-playing players (#147).**

> **727.1** … All players **in that game when it ended** then start a new game …
>
> **729.2** Each player takes all the cards in their main-game library, moves them
> to their subgame library, and shuffles them.
>
> **729.4** All players not currently in the subgame are considered outside the
> subgame.

Three sites, one shared filter:

- `Setup.startGameFromCards`: `owners = Departure.stillPlaying gs`, not the raw
  `turnOrder`.
- `Setup.restartGame` and `Setup.subgameStateFrom`: the new `turnOrder` is
  `rotateTo starter (filter stillPlaying (turnOrder gs))`.
- `Engine.playSubgame`'s `Prompt.RandomFirstPlayer` candidate list is
  `stillPlaying parent`.

And the duplicated local `resetPlayer` helper (defined identically inside both
`restartGame` and `subgameStateFrom`) becomes one shared `Setup.resetPlayers` that
**resets only still-playing players and preserves a departed player's `Status`**.
Keeping departed players in the `players` map with their status intact — while
excluding them from `turnOrder` — keeps every `Map.lookup` total and makes the
rebuilt game's roster a single source of truth. This is the whole of #147.

### 3.10 What this phase deliberately does *not* touch

- **#132** (inherent monarch triggers placed after the ordered APNAP batch) is
  assessed and stays **out**. It is a CR 603.3b interleaving problem within one
  controller's batch; multiplayer neither causes it nor makes it reachable, and it
  still needs a card that stacks a normal trigger alongside an inherent monarch
  trigger for the same player. Unchanged, card-driven.
- **Combat damage assignment order.** CR 703.4k and CR 802.5 both say combat
  damage is assigned in APNAP order. pawl's `Damage.gatherCombatDamage` walks
  attackers then blockers, which is not the same thing — but this is a
  **pre-existing** two-player-reachable question (the attacking and defending
  players both assign), not one multiplayer creates. Filed as its own issue (§8)
  rather than absorbed into this phase's scope.

### 3.11 Capabilities, not format names — the seam without the record

Two rules in this phase's scope (CR 103.5c's free first mulligan, CR 103.8c's
first-turn draw) are things a *later* format would also want to grant. That raises
the question of how pawl should eventually model game types, and it is worth
settling the *shape* now even though nothing is built.

**The CR already factors this way, and the factoring is explicit:**

> **800.2** These rules consist of a series of **options** that can be added to a
> multiplayer game and a number of **variant** styles of multiplayer play. A
> single game may use multiple options but only one variant.

Composable options (CR 801–805) plus exactly one variant (CR 806–811). A named
variant is a *preset over options*, not a primitive — CR 806.2 says so directly
for free-for-all ("The Free-for-All variant uses the following default options"),
listing 806.2a/806.2b/806.2c as option settings rather than as behaviour.

Brawl is the same story one level down:

> **903.12a** Brawl is an **option** for a different style of Commander game.
> Brawl games use the normal rules for the Commander variant **with the following
> modifications**.

and the glossary agrees ("An option for the Commander casual variant"). Its
modifications are a capability list, not a behaviour: CR 903.12f (starting life 25
two-player / 30 multiplayer), CR 903.12g (free first mulligan), CR 903.12h ("Brawl
games do not use the state-based action described in rule 704.6c").

**The load-bearing example is the one this phase actually implements.** Two
independent rules grant the *same* capability for *different* reasons — CR 800.6
(multiplayer) and CR 903.12g (Brawl) — and CR 103.5c is nothing but their union,
stated by name:

> **103.5c** In a multiplayer game **and in any Brawl game**, the first mulligan a
> player takes doesn't count toward the number of cards that player will put on
> the bottom of their library or the number of mulligans that player may take.

A model keyed on format *names* has to write that disjunction at every site that
consumes the capability, and has to rewrite every one of them when a third cause
appears. A model keyed on the *capability* writes it once. That is the argument —
the CR's own factoring plus this example — and it is not an argument about the
closed/open invariant. A `Variant` sum type would be perfectly legitimate under
that invariant: CLAUDE.md is explicit that casing on a keyword or a `Phase` is not
a violation because those are the rulebook's own vocabulary, and CR 800.2's "only
one variant" makes `Variant` exactly that kind of closed enumeration.

**What this phase does: name the seam. What it does not do: build the record.**

Free-for-all uses **zero** options; every one sits at its default (CR 806.2a
declines limited range of influence, 806.2c declines deploy creatures, and §2.2
covers 806.2b). A settings record whose every field is a constant that no site
reads is speculative generality, and the project's sequencing rule is to retire
risk rather than to add unused structure. So there is no `Options` record, no
`Variant` type, and no `GameState` field for either.

What there *is*: every capability this phase needs is derived through **one named
function** whose name states the capability, not the cause — `freeMulligans`
(§3.9) and `skipsDraw` (§3.9). The player-count test lives inside the function.
When a format layer arrives it redefines those functions and touches no call site.
That costs nothing today and is the whole of the benefit; the recommendation for
what the eventual record should look like is recorded in §8 so it is inherited
rather than rediscovered.

**Reconciling this with `freeMulligans :: GameState -> Natural` (§3.9).** A reader
will notice an apparent contradiction: this section declines the options record on
speculative-generality grounds, while §3.9 picks a return type that admits values
no CR rule can currently produce. The distinguishing factor is **cost**, and it is
worth stating so neither decision reads as arbitrary.

Choosing a function's return type is **free and reversible**: same function, same
call site, no new type, no new module, no `GameState` field, no codec arm, and a
one-line change back if the shape ever proves wrong. The options record is
**structural and load-bearing**: a new type, a field on `GameState`, a codec arm, a
serialization decision, and a decision about where the value is set and who may
change it — none of which can be walked back cheaply once card data and saved
states depend on it.

The rule both decisions apply is the same: **free and reversible gets the benefit
of the doubt; structural and load-bearing does not.** That asymmetry is why the
`Natural` is chosen on its own merits (§3.9's two reasons, neither of which is
"a future format might want 2") while the record waits for a customer.

## 4. The three-player test fixture

This is the falsifier for everything above, so it is designed here rather than
improvised in the plan.

**The existing two-player suite is not generalized.** Two-player Magic is a
supported, correct configuration with its own rules (CR 102.2, CR 103.8a) and
~2,000 assertions across 33 spec files that describe it accurately. Rewriting them
to three seats would delete the two-player coverage to buy multiplayer coverage,
and would touch essentially the whole suite. A third seat is **added alongside**.

**`Pawl.Support` gains:**

```haskell
carol :: PlayerId          -- MkPlayerId 2
threePlayers :: NonEmpty PlayerId   -- alice :| [bob, carol]
```
plus, mirroring the existing two-player builders one-for-one:

- `threeWayMirror :: Registry -> IO (NonEmpty (PlayerId, Deck))` — `Setup.mirror`
  over `threePlayers`; `Setup.mirror` is already `NonEmpty`-shaped, so this is
  three lines.
- `threePlayerLandsOnly :: Registry -> IO (NonEmpty (PlayerId, Deck))` — the
  three-seat twin of `landsOnly`, and the input to the headline property below.
- `threePlayerCombat :: … -> (GameState, …)` — the three-seat twin of
  `combatBoardOf`: alice active with attackers, bob and carol each with a
  creature and each a legal defending-player choice.
- `threePlayerGame :: GameState` — `Setup.emptyGame threePlayers`, the base for
  unit-level departure and turn-order tests.

**The scripted interpreters are the enforcement mechanism.** `identityAnswer`,
`castAnswer`, `aggressiveAnswer`, `playLandAnswer` and `randomAnswer` all match
`Prompt` exhaustively, so adding `Prompt.ChooseDefender` makes the `-Werror`
build fail until every interpreter has an arm. Each gets a deliberate answer
(`identityAnswer`: the first candidate; `aggressiveAnswer`: the first candidate;
`randomAnswer`: draws from the `StdGen`, like `RandomFirstPlayer`), and each is a
recorded decision rather than a default.

**Where the tests live.** No new spec module: the convention is
`Pawl.Foo ↔ Pawl.FooSpec`, and there is no `Pawl.Multiplayer` module. Unit-level
tests go in the mirror spec of the module under test — `ExpirySpec`, `CombatSpec`,
`DamageSpec`, `MulliganSpec`, `SetupSpec`, `DepartureSpec`, and — for the monarch,
which has no spec module of its own — `ResolveSpec`, `ExpirySpec` and
`SetupSpec`, where the existing monarch tests already live. The gameplay-level
three-player scenarios go in `GameSpec`, which is already the integration home.

**Two property tests are generalized — the only two that should be.**
`Pawl.PropertySpec` has exactly two properties over whole games, both driven by
two-player matchups:

- *"every matchup upholds every universal invariant"* — add `threeWayMirror` to
  `S.matchups`. Every invariant that holds at two seats must hold at three; this
  is the cheapest possible broad falsifier and it costs one list entry.
- *"a lands-only mirror always ends by deck-out"* — add a three-seat variant. **At
  three players the first deck-out must not end the game.** This single property
  exercises the seat walk (§3.2), the priority handoff (§3.3), CR 800.4a's object
  removal (§3.4), the pass count (§3.3) and `outcomeAfterLeaving` (§3.5) at once,
  and it is the property this phase should be judged by.

`TurnSpec`'s "a turn never revisits a phase" is phase-shaped, not player-shaped,
and needs no change. The `SlugSpec` and `DamageSpec` properties are unrelated.

## 5. Tests and falsifiers, by piece

Named after rule numbers, per `docs/design.md` §4's "free coverage map."

**CR 800.4k / 800.4m / #87 (`ExpirySpec`, `GameSpec`).** Bob arms an "until your
next turn" effect; Bob departs; the handoff walks alice → *bob's seat* → carol.
Assert the effect is gone at carol's turn, present before it, and that
`activePlayer` is never bob. A second test with two consecutive departed seats
proves the walk is a loop and not a single skip.

**CR 800.4a last sentence / #143 (`GameSpec`).** Bob concedes while holding
priority; assert `GameState.priority == Just carol`, not `Just alice`, and
`passes == 0`.

**CR 800.4j (`GameSpec`).** Alice concedes during alice's own turn; assert the
turn continues (phase advances), `activePlayer` is still alice, and priority is
bob's.

**CR 800.4a objects / Master Thief (`ResolveSpec`, `GameSpec`).** Both
directions, as in §0. Assert the artifact's controller after each departure and
that Master Thief's object is gone in the first case.

**CR 800.4b / Mindslaver (`ResolveSpec`).** A departed player cannot be targeted
for control, and a Mindslaver controller departing releases their victim before
the victim's turn begins.

**CR 800.4d (`TriggerSpec`).** A trigger controlled by a departed player is not
put on the stack.

**CR 800.4e (`DamageSpec`).** The defending player concedes between declare
blockers and combat damage; no damage is assigned to them.

**CR 507.1 / 703.4h / 725.2 / Palace Jailer (`CombatSpec`, `GameSpec`).** The
milestone's sharpest test: alice is the monarch; bob attacks. Attacking alice with
an unblocked creature makes bob the monarch (CR 725.2); attacking carol does not.
Both runs share every input but the answer to `Prompt.ChooseDefender`. A third run
asserts the prompt is *not* issued when only one opponent remains.

**CR 509.1 (`CombatSpec`).** Only the chosen defending player is asked to declare
blockers; the third player is never prompted, and their untapped creatures do not
appear in any candidate list.

**CR 725.4 / #128 (`GameSpec`).** The monarch departs on a turn that is not
theirs → the active player is the monarch. The monarch departs on their own turn
→ the next still-playing seat. The last two players depart → no monarch, no crash.

**CR 103.5c / #148 (`MulliganSpec`).** Three players: the first mulligan bottoms
zero, the second bottoms one. Two players: unchanged, the first bottoms one.

**CR 103.8c / 800.7 (`GameSpec`, `TurnSpec`).** Three players: the starting
player draws on turn one. Two players: unchanged, they do not.

**CR 727.1 / 729.2 / #147 (`SetupSpec`).** A restart and a subgame after a
departure each rebuild with two seats, not three; the departed player has no
library, no opening hand, no seat in `turnOrder`, and cannot be rolled as the
starting player.

**CR 104.2a / 104.4a (`DepartureSpec`).** Three players, one departs → no result.
Two remaining, one departs → `Won`. Three departing simultaneously → `Drawn`.

**The `Opponent` axis (`PlayerEffectSpec`, `CastSpec`, `CountSpec`).** There is no
`TargetSpec` module in the suite; targeting is covered from `CastSpec` and
`ResolveSpec`. Silence silences two; Palace Jailer's target pool spans two boards; a
`Relative Opponent` count folds two zones.

**Regression.** Every existing two-player assertion is unchanged. The
`Prompt.ChooseDefender` elision at one candidate is what guarantees this, and a
test asserts the elision explicitly rather than inferring it from the suite
staying green.

### The shape the format/variant work should take (carry this into the issues)

This paragraph belongs verbatim in **both** the CR 801–805 options issue and
#148's Brawl half, so the reasoning is inherited rather than rediscovered.

When options and variants are eventually built, model them as **a typed settings
record plus a `Variant` selection** — not as a flat sum of format names. The CR
factors itself that way and says so: CR 800.2 ("a series of **options** that can be
added … and a number of **variant** styles … A single game may use multiple options
but only one variant"), CR 806.2 (free-for-all *is* a set of default option
settings), CR 903.12a and the Brawl glossary entry ("Brawl is an **option** for a
different style of Commander game … with the following modifications"). A named
format is a **preset over options**, and the preset is where the name belongs.

The decisive evidence is CR 103.5c: **one capability with two independent causes.**
CR 800.6 grants the free first mulligan because the game is multiplayer; CR 903.12g
grants it because the game is Brawl; CR 103.5c is their union stated by name. A
name-keyed model must write that disjunction at every consuming site and rewrite
every one when a third cause appears. This phase therefore routes it through a
single `freeMulligans` (§3.9, §3.11), which is the seam that record slots into.

**Capabilities are not booleans, so this is a typed record and not a flag set** —
the project forbids boolean blindness. CR 903.12f sets a starting life total of
*25 or 30* depending on player count; CR 903.12h **removes** a state-based action
(CR 704.6c's commander damage) rather than toggling behaviour; CR 801.2a's range of
influence is a number of seats that may differ per player. Each of those is a typed
field or a typed absence, not a `Bool`.

Do **not** argue this from the closed/open invariant. A `Variant` sum type is
legitimate under it — CLAUDE.md is explicit that casing on a keyword or a `Phase`
is not a violation, because those are the rulebook's own vocabulary, and CR 800.2's
"only one variant" makes `Variant` the same kind of closed enumeration. The case
for capabilities is the CR's factoring and the mulligan example, not purity.

## 11. Open questions, and decisions I made for you

This spec was written without a human in the loop, so every question that would
have been asked was instead decided and recorded here. Any of these can be
overruled; the ones marked **(structural)** would change §3 if reversed.

1. **The milestone label `M5.6`.** Chosen to match the M3.5 / M4.5 / M5.5
   interstitial pattern — an interstitial between M5.5 and M6 that does not
   renumber M6–M7. `M5.7` and a plain `M6` (renumbering the transpiler to M7) were
   the alternatives. Naming call; overrule freely.
2. **CR 806.2b (§2.2) — implementing the 506.2a/507.1 base choice rather than one
   of the mandatory 802/803 options.** (structural) The alternative was
   implementing attack-left, which is a *smaller* change (no prompt at all at
   three seats — your left-hand neighbour is forced) but which builds a variant
   toggle's semantics without the toggle, and which would elide a choice the
   unrestricted rule makes real. I chose the version that produces a prompt.
3. **`passes` is reset on concession (§3.3).** The CR does not say. I recorded
   both readings and chose the one that cannot silently deny a player a response.
   If you disagree, #143's second half should be closed as "not a defect" with
   that reasoning rather than left open.
4. **`Result` is not widened (§3.5).** (structural) I read CR 104.1/104.2a/104.4a
   as giving a free-for-all exactly one game outcome, with the per-player outcome
   living in `Player.status`. If you want a per-player result type anyway — for
   an interpreter's benefit rather than the rules' — that is a different design
   and belongs in M7.
5. **`turnOrder` is the permanent seating roster (§3.1).** (structural) Three
   rules depend on it. Pruning on departure is the alternative and it makes CR
   800.4m, CR 800.4a's priority clause, and CR 729.1b's non-winner set all
   impossible.
6. **"Target opponent" is deferred rather than built (§3.7).** No card in the pool
   prints it, so building it now would violate the project's card-driven
   discipline — but it is one atom and one `View` field, and a reasonable person
   could argue it belongs in a milestone whose whole subject is what "opponent"
   means. I recorded the exact shape so deferring costs nothing. **Filed as #168**,
   naming Cruel Edict ({1}{B} Sorcery, "Target opponent sacrifices a creature of
   their choice", verified against Scryfall) as the gate card.
7. **The existing two-player suite is not generalized; a third seat is added
   alongside (§4).** The alternative touches ~2,000 assertions in 33 files and
   deletes the two-player coverage. I am confident about this one.
8. **`Prompt.ChooseDefender` is elided at one candidate (§3.6)** with no expiry,
   on the "where the rules leave nothing to ask, don't prompt" reading. This is
   also what keeps every two-player test unchanged, so the two motivations agree —
   which is worth noticing, because it means the elision would survive even if the
   reading were wrong, and a reviewer should check the reading on its own terms.
   **Filed as #169.** No card can expire it — CR 703.4h fires only in a multiplayer
   game (two-player CR 506.2 makes the defender automatic, so nothing is elided
   there at all), and what retires it is CR 802/803 or #59, none of them a
   printing. Re-verify the reading after M5.6d lands.
9. **CR 800.4a is implemented in full; 800.4c/f/g/h/i are deferred (§3.4, §8).**
   The line is "does the pool contain a card that can reach it." I am least sure
   about 800.4h (a *rules*-required choice by a departed player), which needs no
   card at all — but after 800.4a a departed player owns nothing and controls
   nothing, so every rules-required choice they could face is vacuous. If that
   reasoning is wrong, 800.4h belongs in M5.6c.
10. **"A player who *can* become the monarch" (CR 725.4) is read as "is still in
    the game" (§3.8).** No pool card restricts it.
11. **Restart and subgame keep departed players in the `players` map with their
    `Status` preserved, and out of `turnOrder` (§3.9).** The alternative — deleting
    them from `players` — makes lookups partial for any dangling `PlayerId`.
12. **No options/settings record is built, but the capability seam is named
    (§3.11).** (structural) Free-for-all uses zero options and every one sits at
    its default, so a record whose fields nothing reads would be speculative
    generality. Instead each capability is derived through one named function
    (`freeMulligans`, `skipsDraw`) that states the capability rather than the
    cause, and §8 records the shape the eventual record should take. The
    alternative — building the record now — is the one I'd expect to be argued
    for, and the reason to decline is sequencing, not taste: nothing reads it
    yet, and the seam captures the entire benefit at zero cost. §3.11's closing
    paragraph states the cost asymmetry that keeps this consistent with the next
    two decisions.
13. **`freeMulligans` returns `Natural`, not `Bool` (§3.9).** Justified by
    no-boolean-blindness and by the call site being subtraction — **not** by any
    prospect of a rule granting two free mulligans. I checked: the CR has exactly
    three free-mulligan rules (103.5c, 800.6, 903.12g), all worded "the *first*
    mulligan," and none grants a second. The type admits 2 and nothing produces
    it, which is fine because the choice is free and reversible.
14. **`Engine.skipsDraw` stays a bare `Bool` (§3.9).** The opposite call from 13,
    on purpose: it is a proposition whose name *is* the proposition, consumed by a
    single `Monad.unless`, and CR 103.8a/b/c partition the space with no third
    state. `Filter.matches`, `Target.stillLegal` and `Condition.holds` set the
    local precedent. Changing it would cost a new `Pawl.Type.*` module for one
    `unless`. Recorded rather than changed silently — if you want the sum type,
    it is a one-file edit and belongs in M5.6b.
15. **Combat damage assignment APNAP order is filed, not fixed (§3.10).** It is a
    pre-existing two-player-reachable divergence and absorbing it would grow this
    phase for a reason unrelated to multiplayer. If you would rather fix it while
    the combat code is open, it belongs in M5.6d. **Filed as #170.**

### The §3.8 edge is settled — it is CR 800.4i

An earlier draft of this spec recorded the Palace Jailer departure edge as
unsettleable from the rules text: a Palace Jailer effect whose controller leaves
the game while a creature is still exiled under it. It is settleable, and the
answer is a rule this spec had already deferred as card-driven (§8).

**The effect survives its controller's departure.** CR 800.4a ends only "effects
which give that player control of any objects or players"; the exile grants no
control. And Palace Jailer exiles "target creature an opponent controls", so the
exiled card is not owned by the departing player and 800.4a's "all objects owned
by that player leave the game" does not reach it. The card's own ruling
corroborates that the watch is not tied to the source object:

> Palace Jailer leaving the battlefield won't cause the exiled creature to
> return. The game will continue to watch for the next time an opponent becomes
> the monarch. *(2021-03-19)*

**The opponent-set is resolved by last known information.** The return condition
needs to know who the departed controller's opponents are, which is information
about a specific player:

> **800.4i** If an effect requires information about a specific player, the effect
> uses the current information about that player if they are still in the game;
> otherwise, the effect uses the **last known information** about that player
> before they left the game.

So the set freezes at departure. In a free-for-all with no teams that set is every
other player who was in the game, which reduces to a clean operational rule:
**after the controller departs, any remaining player becoming the monarch returns
the card.** That agrees with the card's ruling that "any opponent becoming the
monarch will cause the card to return" — the identity of the opponent never
mattered.

**And there is an immediate consequence worth one test.** If the departing
controller *was* the monarch, CR 725.4 crowns a new monarch at the same time they
leave — the active player, or the next player in turn order if the active player
is the one leaving. That new monarch is necessarily in the frozen set, so the
exiled creature returns in the same departure. One scenario exercises monarch
reassignment (#128), CR 800.4i, and the exile return together.

**Scope consequence.** §8 defers CR 800.4i as card-driven, "the first effect
reading a specific player's information." This is that case, and it arrives inside
M5.6c — so CR 800.4i moves out of §8 and into M5.6c's scope. It is the narrow
form only: the departed player's opponent-set, not a general last-known-information
facility.

**A live defect found on the way (#171, filed, not absorbed).**
`Monarch.returnExiledForMonarch` is a state check — an entry is due iff its
controller is not the *current* monarch — but the ruling requires an **event**:

> If you're not the monarch as Palace Jailer's second ability resolves, the
> creature will be exiled until there's a new monarch and that player is one of
> your opponents. **The creature won't immediately return just because an opponent
> is the monarch.** *(2021-03-19)*

Reachable at two players by ordering Palace Jailer's two ETB triggers (CR 603.3b)
so the exile resolves while an opponent is already the monarch. Not
multiplayer-created and not absorbed here, the same posture taken for #170. The
M5.6c departure path is unaffected, because a CR 725.4 reassignment *is* a new
monarch being crowned, so both readings agree there.
